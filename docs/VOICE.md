# Good Bot, Bad Bot: voice and copy guide

Every word on the site follows this guide. Read it before you write or change any text.

## Who is talking
A warm, playful friend who knows a lot about AI design and loves sharing it. Think "a kind game host",
not "a teacher" and not "a brand". The friend is on the player's side, always.

## The 4 rules, in order
1. **Clear first.** The player never has to stop and think "what does this mean?" or "what do I do now?".
   One idea per sentence. Short sentences (aim for under 15 words). Common words.
2. **Tell people what to do.** Start instructions with a verb: "Tap the better screen." Buttons say what
   happens: "Next round", "Save my card", not "OK" or "Continue".
3. **Feel with the player.** Name the moment: a win feels like a win, a miss feels safe, a wait feels short.
   Never blame. Never make the player feel slow.
4. **Then add the spark.** A little wit, a vivid verb, a small surprise. Only after 1-3 are true.
   Creative is good; clever-but-unclear is not.

## Moments and how they should feel
| Moment | Feeling we want | Example |
|---|---|---|
| First visit, empty state | Welcome, curious, "this is easy" | "No cards yet. Your first one is one game away." |
| Instructions | Calm, confident | "Tap the better AI screen. That's it." |
| Right answer | Proud, a quick high five | "Nailed it!", "Sharp eye!", "That's the one." |
| Wrong answer | Safe, still learning, not stupid | "So close. Here's the trick:", "Easy to miss. Look at…" |
| Streak / combo | Excited, on fire | "3 in a row. You're on fire!" |
| Results, great | Celebrate | "Perfect round. Frame it." |
| Results, low | Encourage, give a next step | "Tough one. Each miss teaches you a pattern. Again?" |
| Giving up / reveal | No shame | "No worries. Here are the answers." |
| Losing a streak / missed day | Gentle | "Your streak took a nap. Start a new one today." |
| Errors | Honest, calm, a way out | "Something broke on our side. Try again, or go home." |
| Settings / data | Plain and trustworthy | "Your progress stays on this device. We never see it." |

## Words we always use (one word for one thing)
- **XP** (points that level you up), **level**, **card** (a pattern you collected), **stars** (1-3 per card),
  **round**, **streak** = answers right in a row, **day streak** = days played in a row, **skip day**,
  **trust** (in Stories), **brief** → say "your job".
- Say "AI screen", "AI helper" or "the AI". Avoid jargon. If a design word is the point of the lesson
  (like "undo" or "sources"), use it, and explain it in plain words the first time.
- Pattern names are fine as titles. In sentences, say what they do.

## Words and habits to avoid
- Course words: "lesson", "module", "learn about", "in this section", "students", "curriculum".
- Blame: "Wrong!", "You failed", "Incorrect". Use "Not quite", "So close", "Easy to miss".
- Fillers: "simply", "just" (when it hides effort), "basically", "please note", "in order to".
- Jargon nobody asked for: affordance, heuristic, mental model, latency, provenance, paradigm, leverage.
- Long dashes used as decoration, sentences in brackets, ALL CAPS shouting, more than one emoji in a line.
- Long text. If it needs two sentences, check if one will do.

## Checklist for every string
- Can a 12-year-old who reads English as a second language understand it the first time?
- Does it tell the player what to do or what just happened?
- Does it match the feeling of the moment (table above)?
- Is it the same word we use for this thing everywhere else?
- Is there a spark (a vivid word, a wink) and is it still clear?
- Does it fit on a phone (390 px wide) without ugly wrapping?
