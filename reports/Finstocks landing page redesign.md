# Rebuild FinStocks AI's page around verifiable trust

FinStocks AI's landing page should be redesigned so that trust comes first and the prompt stays the hero. The prompt mechanic ("Invest ₹10,000 now" → AI strategy → execution) is distinctive, easy to grasp in five seconds, and worth keeping at the centre of the page. The trust layer around it, however, looks close to empty in public: no SEBI registration, broker partner or pricing shows up in any indexed source, and the copy relies on superlatives ("India's leading", "world's first", "Ultimate") that SEBI's Advertisement Code bans for registered advisers and analysts. Those gaps matter more than layout, for three reasons. The product asks retail users to connect a real broker account and let an AI trade. Indian households overwhelmingly prefer capital preservation. And the stock-tips space is soaked in WhatsApp and Telegram scams whose playbook (guaranteed returns, profit screenshots, urgency) is exactly what hype-leaning fintech copy resembles. The recommended redesign has four parts. First, strip the unverifiable claims. Second, make Mock Mode (virtual money) the primary call to action. Third, show a prompt-to-rules-to-backtest demo that works without signup. Fourth, place a checkable regulatory and broker strip beside the CTA. Before any of this ships, FinStocks has to confirm which SEBI category it falls under, because the 2025 retail algo framework requires Research Analyst registration for "black-box" strategies, and "Autopilot" may count as one. **An important limit on this report: finstocks.ai could not be fetched.** Every request (curl, WebFetch, a Playwright render) was refused by the research environment's network egress policy with HTTP 403. So the audit of the current page rests entirely on search-engine titles and snippets, not on the rendered page, its DOM, its visuals or its CTAs.

## The current page sells a sharp idea but hides everything a cautious investor checks

### What could and could not be observed

Nobody on the research team saw the live page. finstocks.ai, the Play Store, Trustpilot, Medium, Tracxn, ScamAdviser and web.archive.org were all blocked by the egress proxy. Most competitor sites were blocked as well (Tickertape, Danelfin, Simply Wall St, Composer, Streak, Public, smallcase), and so were sebi.gov.in and the primary sources for Unbounce, Baymard, CXL and web.dev. Nothing in this report about FinStocks' hero headline, CTA labels, section order, testimonials, footer, disclaimers, visual style, tech stack or performance is an observation. Findings marked "snippet" come from search-index summaries and should be treated as approximate wording.

### What the indexed footprint says

The indexed evidence describes an Indian, prompt-based trading and automation platform. The root page's title reads "FinStocks AI | Prompt-based trading strategies on your own ..." (truncated in the index), while /home carries a different title, "FinStocks AI | The Ultimate AI Stock Trading & Investment Platform" ([finstocks.ai](https://finstocks.ai/), [finstocks.ai/home](https://finstocks.ai/home)). The core promise, taken from snippets, is that users "simply type a prompt—'Invest ₹10,000 now'—and the AI handles everything: strategy, execution, and rebalancing in real time." Around that promise sit **Autopilot, a custom strategy builder, backtesting, and a "Mock Mode" to "invest virtual money risk-free before going live"** ([finstocks.ai](https://finstocks.ai/), [LinkedIn](https://in.linkedin.com/company/finstocks-ai)). Upstox is the one broker named for execution ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/finstocks/__RmNKJf4h66SjRkiHKBCT9Vy7iqRdGqhdGgkl__yO3IE)). A /how-to-use page is indexed as "Use Cases & Broker Setup" ([finstocks.ai/how-to-use](https://finstocks.ai/how-to-use)). The company is very young: **founded in 2025 by Rushikesh Chavan in Bengaluru, unfunded, about 5 employees** ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/finstocks/__RmNKJf4h66SjRkiHKBCT9Vy7iqRdGqhdGgkl__yO3IE), [RocketReach](https://rocketreach.co/finstocks-ai-profile_b695ee15c94aa5e2)).

### Contradictions and inconsistencies

The messaging contradicts itself in ways a redesign has to settle. The site title implies users trade "on your own" broker account. Yet the mission line promises "no brokers, no signals, no manual execution," and the founder's Medium post says the AI executes trades "without needing brokers or manual steps" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@founder_50249/how-finstocks-ai-is-changing-the-world-of-stock-market-trading-3eadf5005ee1), snippet only). In India, where algo orders must route through a registered broker, "no brokers" reads either as inaccurate or as unregulated. Snippets also say the product trades "during market hours (9:30–3:30)". That does not match the standard NSE/BSE session opening, which the audit notes flag as 9:15 (a background-knowledge check, not a cited finding). Either the product deliberately skips the opening minutes or the copy is imprecise.

The SEO layer is inconsistent too. Four title formulas appear across indexed pages ("Prompt-based…", "The Ultimate…", "India's Leading AI Trading Platform" on /login, and "AI-Powered Stock Trading Platform | Intelligent Investment Solutions" on /shared URLs). /how-to-use is indexed under two different titles ([finstocks.ai/how-to-use](https://finstocks.ai/how-to-use)). That pattern suggests a client-rendered single-page app with a shared default title that each route overwrites. This is an inference that needs confirmation from source.

### The trust footprint

The trust footprint is thin and noisy. Trustpilot shows a **TrustScore of 3.7 from a single review** ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/finstocks.ai), snippet). ScamAdviser's algorithm gives the domain "a very low trust score" ([ScamAdviser](https://www.scamadviser.com/check-website/finstocks.ai), snippet), which is typical for a young domain and is not evidence of wrongdoing. Searches for a FinStocks SEBI registration or disclaimer returned only an unrelated SEBI-registered analyst, ABJ Finstocks ([multibaggers.co.in](https://multibaggers.co.in/mandatory-disclosures/)). Name collisions compound the problem. The Google Play app called "Finstocks" (co.diy7.krdcm) is an education app from "Education DIY7 Media", last updated in August 2024 ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/finstocks/co.diy7.krdcm)). The notes disagree on this point: the competitor note lists it as FinStocks' app, but the more specific audit evidence says it belongs to someone else, so treat it as not FinStocks' app until confirmed. Finstock, Inc. ([finstock.co](https://www.finstock.co/)) and FinStok AI ([finstok.ai](https://finstok.ai/)) are separate products. Acquisition appears to be founder- and community-led: Luma meetups, Instagram creator reels and a YouTube channel ([Luma](https://luma.com/9xhp34u0), [Instagram reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DR1M-6sk1lW/), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@FinstocksAI)). Many visitors will therefore arrive warm but will immediately look for proof that the platform is legitimate.

### The 5-second read

The net assessment, inferred from indexed copy, is that a visitor probably understands the page in five seconds ("type a sentence and AI invests my money in Indian stocks"). What the visitor cannot find is an answer to the three questions that decide the signup: **who holds my money, which broker executes, and is this allowed by SEBI?**

## Winning competitors pair one try-before-signup artifact with plainly stated regulation

FinStocks is not a research screener. Its real peers are products that go from strategy to execution. **Composer by SoFi** is the closest global analog. It is described as "Build, backtest and execute trading algorithms with AI" ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/composer-2)). Users "explain your goals, strategy, and risk concerns in natural language" and it "executes your trading strategy, making trades and rebalancing automatically" ([composer.trade](https://www.composer.trade/)). Its June 2026 relaunch framed AI credibility in a way FinStocks should borrow directly. The launch release said: "Unlike agentic trading tools that rely on AI to continuously make trading decisions, Composer uses AI to help investors build sophisticated rules-based strategies that are executed automatically according to clear, predefined rules," and it lets investors "evaluate historical performance across different market environments before deciding whether to activate" ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260623095057/en/Introducing-Composer-by-SoFi-AI-Powered-Investing-From-Idea-to-Execution)). Composer's funnel is a **free tier to build and backtest indefinitely**, with automated execution behind a paid "Trading Pass" ([tooliverse](https://tooliverse.ai/tools/composer)).

**Public.com's Generated Assets** leads with a prompt bar ("It all starts with a prompt") and concrete example prompts such as "CEOs under 40". It backtests against the S&P 500 and explains that a "swarm of AI evaluation agents" scores sector fit, financials and growth ([Public on X](https://x.com/public/status/1924480558761152976), [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/investors-can-turn-ideas-into-an-investable-index-with-publics-launch-of-generated-assets-302454862.html)). **Surmount** turns objection-handling into headline copy ("no minimums, no coding, and no giving up control, for free to start"). It also discloses its structure plainly: it is "not a registered investment adviser" and advice comes through an SEC-registered subsidiary ([surmount.ai](https://surmount.ai/)).

The Indian peers borrow trust from brokers. **Zerodha Streak** ("Algo trade without coding") deploys on the user's own Kite account and has been free for Zerodha users since January 2024, capped at **5 live and 15 virtual deployments** ([Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/streak/introducing-streak-algo-trade-without-coding), [tradersunited](https://tradersunited.org/blog/zerodha-streak-review-algo-trading)). **smallcase** runs one idea-led headline, "Invest in ideas", on every broker-hosted instance and states that "all smallcases are managed by SEBI-registered investment experts" ([smallcase.zerodha.com](https://smallcase.zerodha.com/), [smallcase on SBI Securities](https://smallcases.sbisecurities.in/manager/smallcaseHQ)). **Tradetron** proves scale with operating numbers: "11k algos… 1.5 million trades every month" ([Tradetron](https://tradetron.tech/market-place)).

Research-first products show how to talk about AI accuracy without overreaching. **Danelfin** frames its score as a probability over a fixed horizon and discloses both tails: "+21.05%" for 10/10 scores against "-33.28%" for 1/10, since 2017 and against a named benchmark. It also makes scores free for any visitor ([danelfin.com](https://danelfin.com/), [Danelfin Help Center](https://support.danelfin.com/hc/en-us/articles/4405443156625-Why-are-the-AI-Scores-available-for-free)). That style of performance claim works in the US but would be restricted on an Indian RA/IA page (see the next-but-one section). Simply Wall St publishes a no-signup demo portfolio and cites "over 7,000,000 investors" ([demo](https://simplywall.st/portfolio/demo), [simplywall.st](https://simplywall.st/)). Robinhood Cortex proves value with user satisfaction ("95%… love using the product") rather than returns ([Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/markets/tech/25/08/47252402/robinhood-unveils-cortex-ai-tool-digests-in-uk-bringing-real-time-stock-explanations-to-everyday-investors-after-95-us-approval)).

The pattern across the strongest pages is consistent: **a plain-language promise, one explorable artifact a visitor can use before signing up, a free or virtual entry point, a real scale number, and a regulatory line**. The differentiator is the artifact. FinStocks already owns the pieces for one, namely the prompt, backtesting and Mock Mode, but nothing indexed suggests the landing page lets a visitor use them before creating an account. None of these competitor pages could be rendered either. Their verbatim H1s, CTA labels and section orders are unverified; only page titles, press copy and third-party reviews were available.

## Conversion evidence favors a plain, fast, mobile-first hero with proof beside the button

### Attention concentrates in the hero

Attention is front-loaded. NN/g's eyetracking found users spend **about 57% of viewing time above the fold and 74% in the first two screenfuls** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). Users decide within roughly 10 seconds whether to stay ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)). Both studies are older (2018 and 2011). Plain language correlates with conversion: in Unbounce's finance benchmark, pages at a 5th–7th grade reading level had a **median conversion of 11.1%, 56% higher than pages at 8th–9th grade level** ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/finance-insurance-conversion-rate/), via snippet, correlational). For FinStocks this argues against jargon like "alpha" or "multi-factor". It also argues against the US-centric "tools Wall Street keeps for itself" framing found in snippets.

### Proof and demos near the CTA

Proof placed next to the CTA helps, though the evidence comes mostly from single case studies. HubSpot-cited cases report a 68% lift from social proof under the CTA and 124% from "doubt remover" microcopy ([Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro.com/blog/cta-button-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026/)). A test that turned testimonial logos grayscale, so they stopped competing with the form, projected a **34% increase in form submissions** ([VWO](https://vwo.com/blog/ab-testing-examples/)). The popular claim that single-CTA pages beat multi-CTA pages (13.5% vs 10.5%) could not be traced to a primary source. The direction is plausible, but the numbers are unverified. Interactive demos are the one design trend with meaningful (vendor) data: demos above the fold get **3.5x the engagement** of demos below it ([Navattic 2025](https://www.navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2025)).

### Page length and signup friction

Page length should match awareness. In the classic Crazy Egg case, a long-form page beat the short control by 30% because prospects did not understand the category ([Conversion Rate Experts](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/crazy-egg-case-study/)). Prompt-based AI trading is similarly novel, so a longer, scannable page is justified, provided the decision-critical proof sits in the first two or three screens.

Friction evidence favours one-click sign-in and no card. Google-published case studies report **Sign in with Google raising Reddit signups by 50%, and Pinterest Android signups by 126%** ([Google](https://developers.google.com/identity/sign-in/case-studies/reddit), [Google case studies](https://developers.google.com/identity/sign-in/case-studies)). These are vendor-published. Aggregator data suggests card-required trials cut top-of-funnel signups while converting better to paid ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/saas-average-conversion-rate/), [Appcues](https://www.appcues.com/blog/free-to-paid-conversion-strategies)). For FinStocks the natural entry point, Mock Mode, needs neither a card nor a broker connection.

### Speed, mobile and dark mode

Speed has causal support. In Vodafone's A/B test, a **31% LCP improvement produced 8% more sales** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/vodafone)). Google/Deloitte associated a 0.1s mobile speed gain with 8.4% more retail conversions ([Think with Google](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/9757/Milliseconds_Make_Millions_report_hQYAbZJ.pdf)). With **83% of landing-page visits on mobile** in Unbounce's 2024 dataset, and mobile converting about 8% worse than desktop ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/new-report-reveals-an-8-mobile-landing-page-conversion-gap/557513/)), a client-rendered SPA hero is a liability if FinStocks is one, which remains unverified. The "trading terminal" dark aesthetic common in the category carries its own risk: NN/g found light mode gives better reading performance, and the advantage grows as font size shrinks ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)).

### Benchmarks

As a loose benchmark, Unbounce reports a 6.6% all-industry median and 3.8% for SaaS ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)). A 3–8% visitor-to-Mock-Mode-signup rate is a reasonable initial target. That figure is an inference; no fintech-specific benchmark exists.

## SEBI rules and scam-weary investors make trust the core design problem

### Which SEBI rulebook applies

The rule most directly tied to FinStocks is SEBI's February 2025 circular on "Safer participation of retail investors in Algorithmic trading", effective from 1 October 2025 after an extension ([TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/highlights?id=85561), [Finsec Law](https://www.finseclaw.com/article/finsec-tracker-on-sebi-issues-guidelines-on-retail-participation-in-algorithmic-trading)). Under it, **algo providers must be empanelled with exchanges and partner with a registered broker (the principal), every algo order carries an exchange-issued identifier, and all algo charges must be disclosed**. Crucially, a provider of **"black-box" strategies, where the logic is not disclosed to the user, must register as a SEBI Research Analyst** and keep a research report for each algorithm ([QuantInsti](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/algorithmic-trading-india/), [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/knowledge-center/online-share-trading/sebi-algo-trading-rules), [NSE FAQ](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ_Retail%20Algo_03112025_NSE.pdf)). An Autopilot that picks and executes strategies the user cannot inspect looks like black-box execution. Whether FinStocks is an RA, an empanelled provider, both or neither decides which rulebook governs the page, and no public record answered that question. SEBI introduced the framework partly because unregistered algo providers were promising guaranteed returns ([TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/highlights?id=85561)).

### What the Advertisement Code requires and bans

If FinStocks is an RA or IA, the Advertisement Code (in force since 1 May 2023) applies to every communication ([SEBI circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/apr-2023/advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-ia-and-research-analysts-ra-_69798.html)). It **requires** the standard warning "Investment in securities market are subject to market risks. Read all the related documents carefully before investing." in at least 10-point font, plus a disclaimer that registration does not guarantee performance ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebis-advertisement-code-guide-investment-advisers-research-analysts.html)). It **bans** the following: guaranteed or risk-free returns; superlatives such as "Best", "No. 1", "Top" and "leading"; the SEBI logo; celebrities; games, contests and prizes; calling something "free" if it carries any condition; and misleading or projection-based testimonials ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-crackdowns-on-misleading-investment-ads-bars-superlative-terms-123040501056_1.html), [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-issues-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-research-analysts/)). SEBI enforces it. It fined an adviser ₹4 lakh over "sensational" YouTube captions ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/ad-code-breach-sebi-imposes-fine-of-rs-4-lakh-on-basant-maheshwari-wealth-125032601073_1.html)).

On that standard, FinStocks' indexed phrases "India's leading" and "Ultimate" are direct problems, and "world's first" is at least risky. The US offers a parallel on AI claims: the SEC penalised Global Predictions for calling itself the "first regulated AI financial advisor" ([MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/240320-sec-targets-ai-washing-with-two-new-settled-cases)).

### Past performance, AI disclosure and finfluencers

Past performance is now tightly channelled. Since SEBI's 30 October 2025 circular, IAs and RAs may share CA- or CMA-certified past performance **only on a client's specific request and one-to-one** ([SEBI circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/oct-2025/ease-of-doing-business-interim-arrangement-for-certified-past-performance-of-investment-advisers-and-research-analysts-prior-to-operationalisation-of-past-risk-and-return-verification-agency-parrv-_97556.html)). The Past Risk and Return Verification Agency (PaRRVA), run by CARE Ratings with NSE, launched commercially on 4 May 2026 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/care-ratings-in-collaboration-with-nse-launches-parrva-platform-126050401090_1.html)). A public headline like "87% accurate" or "₹10,000 became ₹14,000" is therefore the single riskiest element this page could carry.

RAs must also **disclose the extent of AI use to clients** and remain fully liable for AI output ([Compliance Calendar](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/sebi-research-analyst-third-amendment-regulations-2024), [Upstox news](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/financial-regulations/sebi-seeks-transparency-in-ai-tool-usage-by-investment-advisors-research-analysts-what-this-means/article-110679/)). And since August 2024, regulated entities cannot associate with unregistered finfluencers who give advice or make return claims ([BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/investment/story/sebi-tweaks-regulations-for-finfluencers-removes-content-of-over-15000-unregulated-entities-443789-2024-08-31)). That directly affects FinStocks' Instagram creator-reel marketing.

### What Indian investors fear

The audience psychology reinforces all of this. The SEBI Investor Survey 2025 (90,000+ households) found **63% aware of securities products but only about 9.5% investing**, with direct equity penetration at 5.3% ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/two-thirds-of-domestic-households-aware-of-securities-products-sebi-survey-126012100349_1.html)). **About 80% of households, including 79% of Gen-Z households, prefer capital preservation to higher returns** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/indian-households-including-gen-z-remain-risk-averse-sebi-survey-125093001479_1.html)). The barriers are complexity, fear of loss and lack of trust ([Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/sebi-investor-survey-2025-insights)).

Active traders have been burned. **More than 91% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY25**, and net losses reached ₹1,05,603 crore ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)). Scam fear is acute: I4C recorded about **₹4,636 crore lost to stock-trading scams in nine months of 2024**, built on WhatsApp and Telegram "expert tips", fake apps showing fake profits, and "guaranteed returns" ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/news/i4c-warns-investors-against-fake-whatsapp-stock-tips-fake-trading-apps)). SEBI responded with @valid UPI handles and the SEBI Check lookup tool ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/10/04/sebi-introduces-verified-upi-handles-and-sebi-check-to-safeguard-investors/)).

AI itself adds friction. Globally, **74% of retail investors trust human advice over robo advice**, and only 39% would consider AI-driven funds. Younger investors are more open ([CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/-/media/documents/survey/cfa_investor_trust_global_report.pdf)). Research on algorithm aversion identifies opaque "black box" logic as the adoption killer. Transparency about decision logic and the ability to monitor changes are the remedies ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.02555), [Cogent Economics & Finance](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23322039.2025.2571403)).

### Where the legal and psychological requirements meet

The legal requirement and the psychological one therefore point the same way. **Show the rules the AI will follow, show that money stays in the user's own broker account, make regulation checkable, and let people practise with virtual money.** Anything that resembles the scam playbook will be pattern-matched as a scam: profit screenshots, countdown timers, "join our Telegram", unverifiable superlatives.

### Three personas, three sets of objections

Three personas need different reassurance. These personas are inferred from SEBI and CFA data, not from FinStocks' own users. The *cautious first-timer* investing about ₹10,000 needs Mock Mode, plain language and upfront fees. The *algo-curious active trader* needs white-box, editable rules, risk controls (stop-loss, max drawdown, kill switch), the broker list, all charges, and backtest methodology. The *long-term investor* needs rebalancing logic and a portfolio view, not hit rates.

## Redesign priorities: fix legal exposure first, then build the try-it loop

The priorities below follow from the evidence. P0 items are prerequisites. They are cheap and remove regulatory and credibility risk, so shipping a prettier page without them would amplify the problem. P1 items carry the main conversion bets. P2 items refine and scale.

## Conclusion

The central insight is that FinStocks' redesign is a regulatory-positioning project first and a visual project second. The most "conversion-optimized" moves in consumer fintech are exactly the moves that SEBI restricts and that India's scam epidemic has taught investors to distrust: headline returns, win rates, creator hype and urgency. FinStocks' advantage lies elsewhere. A prompt that yields **readable rules, a labelled simulation, and orders that appear in the user's own broker app** gives a loss-averse, scam-wary audience something competitors mostly lack: the ability to verify before trusting. Composer's 2026 repositioning, from "AI trades for you" to "AI builds rules you can inspect", suggests the global category is converging on the same answer. FinStocks' current "AI handles everything" line points the other way.

The practical implication is sequencing. Until the registration and broker facts are confirmed, no design can be finalised, because the trust strip, footer, Autopilot copy and even the demo's permissible output all depend on them. Once they are confirmed, the page can turn compliance into a feature: a checkable SEBI Check link and a "your money never leaves your broker" line are among the few claims FinStocks can make that a scammer cannot copy.
