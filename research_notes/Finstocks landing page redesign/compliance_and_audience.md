# Compliance Constraints and Investor-Audience Psychology for an AI Stock-Research / Trading Landing Page (finstocks.ai)

> Research date: 30 Sep 2026. Not legal advice. Every "must/must not" below should be confirmed by a SEBI-regulatory lawyer or compliance officer before launch, in particular which SEBI registration category FinStocks actually holds (RA, IA, exchange-empanelled algo provider, or none), because that determines which rulebook applies.
>
> Method note: finstocks.ai itself, sebi.gov.in, and several law-firm sites were blocked by the research environment's network proxy, so the product description comes from search-engine snippets of the site and third-party pages, and SEBI rules come from law-firm/press summaries of the circulars (URLs to the primary circulars are given where search surfaced them).

## 1. Which market does finstocks.ai serve, and which regulations apply?

### Takeaway
FinStocks AI is an Indian (Bengaluru) product that lets retail users trade Indian equities through text prompts ("Invest ₹10,000 now"), with an "Autopilot" that handles strategy, execution and rebalancing. That makes it subject to SEBI's rules. The main ones are the 2025 retail algo-trading framework, the RA/IA regulations and Advertisement Code (as soon as it recommends securities or runs undisclosed "black-box" strategies), the finfluencer-association rules, and SEBI's AI-disclosure requirement. The US regime (SEC Marketing Rule, FINRA 2210) matters only as a secondary benchmark.

### Cited Findings
**Product and market**
- Search snippets describe FinStocks AI as "India's leading AI trading platform" and "the world's first prompt-based AI trading platform". Users type prompts such as "Invest ₹10,000 now" and the AI "handles strategy, execution, and rebalancing in real time". Features include Autopilot, custom strategies, backtesting, a "Mock Mode" that uses virtual money, and automatic trading during market hours (9:30–3:30). The site title reads "FinStocks AI | Prompt-based trading strategies on your own ..." — [finstocks.ai (search snippet)](https://finstocks.ai/); [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.diy7.krdcm&hl=en_US)
- The founder is Rushikesh Chavan. The company is based in Bengaluru, was founded in 2025, and had not raised funding according to Tracxn — [LinkedIn](https://in.linkedin.com/company/finstocks-ai); [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/finstocks/__RmNKJf4h66SjRkiHKBCT9Vy7iqRdGqhdGgkl__yO3IE)
- Searches found no public record of a SEBI RA/IA registration number or NSE algo-provider empanelment for FinStocks. This could not be verified because the SEBI and NSE sites were blocked. Other searches showed a Trustpilot page and Instagram/YouTube marketing — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/finstocks.ai); [Instagram reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DR1M-6sk1lW/)

**SEBI retail algo-trading framework (the rule most directly tied to an "AI trading/Autopilot" product)**
- SEBI's circular of 4 Feb 2025, "Safer participation of retail investors in Algorithmic trading", covers algo trading through broker APIs. It originally took effect on 1 Aug 2025 and the deadline was later extended to 1 Oct 2025 — [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/highlights?id=85561); [Finsec Law](https://www.finseclaw.com/article/finsec-tracker-on-sebi-issues-guidelines-on-retail-participation-in-algorithmic-trading); [QuantInsti](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/algorithmic-trading-india/)
- Algo providers must be empanelled with the exchanges and must partner with a registered broker. The broker is the principal and the algo provider is its agent. Every algo order carries an exchange-issued unique identifier. Brokers and algo providers must disclose all algo-related charges — [QuantInsti](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/algorithmic-trading-india/); [Spice Route Legal](https://spiceroutelegal.com/publications/regulating-algo-providers-sebis-norms-on-algorithmic-trading/)
- **White-box vs black-box:** a provider of "black box" strategies, meaning the logic is not disclosed to the user, must register with SEBI as a Research Analyst and keep a research report for each algorithm — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/knowledge-center/online-share-trading/sebi-algo-trading-rules); [QuantInsti](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/algorithmic-trading-india/); NSE's FAQ is at [NSE Retail Algo FAQ (Nov 2025)](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ_Retail%20Algo_03112025_NSE.pdf) and the list of empanelled providers at [NSE empanelled algo providers](https://www.nseindia.com/static/trade/empanelled-algo-providers-exchange)
- SEBI brought in this framework partly because unregistered algo providers were promising guaranteed returns — [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/highlights?id=85561)
- Press guidance on the framework tells retail investors to "trust back-tested data, not hype" — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/algo-trading-by-retail-investors-trust-back-tested-data-not-hype-125072901081_1.html)

**SEBI RA Regulations (amended 16–17 Dec 2024)**
- Primary text: [SEBI RA Regulations, 2014, last amended 16 Dec 2024](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/regulations/dec-2024/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-research-analysts-regulations-2014-last-amended-on-december-16-2024-_90153.html)
- Fees charged to individual and HUF clients are capped at ₹1,51,000 per year per client, excluding statutory charges. The cap is reviewed every three years using the Cost Inflation Index — [Compliance Calendar](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/sebi-research-analyst-third-amendment-regulations-2024); [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-introduces-major-reforms-for-investment-advisers-and-research-analysts/75888.html)
- **AI disclosure:** RAs must tell clients how far AI tools are used in their services, starting at onboarding. Existing clients had to be told by 30 Apr 2025 — [Compliance Calendar](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/sebi-research-analyst-third-amendment-regulations-2024); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-issues-new-guidelines-for-research-analysts-investment-advisers-125010801273_1.html)
- The IA/RA remains fully responsible for AI output, including data security and regulatory compliance. It cannot shift liability onto the algorithm — [Upstox news](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/financial-regulations/sebi-seeks-transparency-in-ai-tool-usage-by-investment-advisors-research-analysts-what-this-means/article-110679/); [Business Standard (Aug 2024 proposal)](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/investment-advisers-analysts-must-disclose-ai-tool-usage-to-clients-sebi-124081500373_1.html)
- Other changes: a part-time RA category with segregation from other businesses, and a lower qualification bar (a graduate degree in any discipline) — [Compliance Calendar](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/sebi-research-analyst-third-amendment-regulations-2024)

**SEBI finfluencer rules (Aug 2024; clarified Jan 2025)**
- From 29 Aug 2024, SEBI-regulated entities may not associate, directly or indirectly, with unregistered persons who give securities advice or recommendations or make claims about returns or performance. "Association" covers paying them, referring clients to them, and letting their tech systems interact with the entity's — [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/investment/story/sebi-tweaks-regulations-for-finfluencers-removes-content-of-over-15000-unregulated-entities-443789-2024-08-31); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/sebi-directs-regulated-entities-to-sever-ties-with-finfluencers-in-3-months); [SEBI board paper](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jul-2024/1719916854117_1.pdf)
- There is an exemption for pure investor education that does not advise, recommend, or make claims about share performance. SEBI clarified in Jan 2025 that "education" content cannot use live price data from the previous 3 months, which was aimed at finfluencers selling stock tips under the label of education — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-finfluencer-circular-live-stock-data-market-education-rules-125013000571_1.html); [News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/sebi-cracks-down-on-finfluencers-selling-stock-tips-in-the-name-of-education)
- The exact 3-month threshold comes from a search snippet and should be verified.

**US (brief, benchmark only)**
- **SEC Marketing Rule 206(4)-1 (registered investment advisers):** since the 2020 rule, testimonials and endorsements are permitted, but they must disclose whether the promoter is a client and whether they were paid, and compensated promoters with disqualifying events in the past 10 years are barred. Hypothetical performance, which includes backtests, is allowed only with policies and procedures ensuring it is relevant to the audience's financial situation, plus disclosure of the underlying assumptions — [SEC compliance guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing); [SEC Marketing FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/division-investment-management-frequently-asked-questions/marketing-compliance-frequently-asked-questions); [Smarsh](https://www.smarsh.com/regulations/sec-marketing-rule-206-4-1). One aggregator snippet claimed testimonials are "prohibited" under 206(4)-1(a)(1). That describes the pre-2020 rule and is outdated.
- **AI-washing enforcement:** in March 2024 the SEC settled with Delphia ($225,000 penalty) and Global Predictions ($175,000) for false claims about their AI. Global Predictions had called itself the "first regulated AI financial advisor" offering "expert AI-driven forecasts" — [Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/investigation-fraud-and-risk/ai-washing-enforcement/); [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/240320-sec-targets-ai-washing-with-two-new-settled-cases)
- **FINRA Rule 2210 (broker-dealers):** FINRA treats AI-generated and chatbot communications as regulated communications with the same content, supervision and recordkeeping standards as any other. The rule has historically barred performance projections. FINRA has proposed amendments to allow limited projections and targeted returns (Regulatory Notice 26-14; Mintz commentary dated July 2026) — [FINRA 2025 oversight report](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/reports/2025-finra-annual-regulatory-oversight-report/communication-with-public); [FINRA RN 26-14](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-14); [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2401/2026-07-14-pivotal-moment-broker-dealer-communications-finra)

### Inferences
- The regulatory status of "Autopilot" is the central legal question for the landing page. If the AI chooses and executes strategies whose logic the user cannot see, that looks like a black-box algo, which requires RA registration, exchange empanelment and a broker partnership. The page should then show the RA registration number, the name of the partner broker, and the exchange algo ID or empanelment. If FinStocks only offers white-box, user-built strategies, it may avoid RA registration, but it would still need to be empanelled through a broker. Legal review is required on this point.
- The phrases "India's leading" and "world's first" appear in FinStocks' own marketing snippets. The Advertisement Code bans them if FinStocks is an RA or IA (see Section 2), and the SEC's Global Predictions case shows that "first … AI advisor" claims draw enforcement attention even in the US. These phrases should be removed from the redesign.
- A prominent "How the AI works / what data it uses / where a human is involved" section would do two jobs: it meets SEBI's AI-extent disclosure duty, and it addresses the black-box trust problem described in Section 3.

### Gaps
- FinStocks' actual registration status (RA/IA number, broker partner, NSE empanelment) could not be confirmed because the SEBI, NSE and finstocks.ai sites were blocked. The team should check this directly on the SEBI intermediary search or the SEBI Check app.
- Whether SEBI's June 2025 consultation on AI/ML guidelines for market participants has been finalised by Sep 2026 was not researched.
- No authoritative source was found on whether the exchanges' algo-provider rules add their own limits on advertising returns, beyond the general ban on return promises.

## 2. What must the page show, and which claims are prohibited or risky?

### Takeaway
Under SEBI's Advertisement Code for IAs and RAs (effective 1 May 2023), the page must show the registration details and fixed risk warnings. It must not show guaranteed or risk-free returns, superlatives ("best", "No. 1", "leading"), the SEBI logo, misleading testimonials, celebrities, prizes or contests, "free" offers that come with conditions, or unverified past performance or accuracy figures. Since Oct 2025, past performance can be shared only one-to-one when a client asks for it, certified by a CA or CMA, with disclaimers. From May 2026 the route is verification by PaRRVA (CARE Ratings with NSE). A headline accuracy percentage such as "87% accurate calls" is the single riskiest element a landing page could carry.

### Cited Findings
**Mandatory elements (Advertisement Code, SEBI circular April 2023)**
- Primary circular: [SEBI – Advertisement code for IA and RA (Apr 2023)](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/apr-2023/advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-ia-and-research-analysts-ra-_69798.html). The code applies to all communications by or on behalf of IAs and RAs from 1 May 2023 — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d1aafbb4-64df-4677-ab75-64b73b6faa14)
- A standard warning in at least 10-point font: "Investment in securities market are subject to market risks. Read all the related documents carefully before investing." — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebis-advertisement-code-guide-investment-advisers-research-analysts.html); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-issues-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-research-analysts/)
- A standard disclaimer that SEBI registration, BASL/IAASB membership and NISM certification do not guarantee performance or assure returns — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebis-advertisement-code-guide-investment-advisers-research-analysts.html)
- Registration details: the name as registered with SEBI and the registration number. This is standard under the code, but the precise wording was not verified against the primary circular because it was blocked.

**Prohibited content**
- Any promise or guarantee of assured or risk-free returns — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/sebi-issues-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-research-analysts-news-276246); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-issues-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-research-analysts/)
- Superlatives such as "Best", "No. 1", "Top", "leading", and "one of the best amongst market leaders" — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-crackdowns-on-misleading-investment-ads-bars-superlative-terms-123040501056_1.html); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-issues-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-research-analysts/)
- Use of the SEBI logo. Use of celebrities to promote. Games, leagues, competitions, prize money, medals or gifts. Calling a service "free" unless it really is free with no condition or obligation — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-issues-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-research-analysts/); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/sebi-issues-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-research-analysts-news-276246)
- Testimonials or statements that are false, misleading, biased, deceptive or presumptive, or that rest on assumptions or projections — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-issues-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-research-analysts/); [Kruti Gogri & Co](https://cskruti.com/all-about-the-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-and-research-analysts/)
- BSE, as the RA administration body, has issued clarifications on the code for RAs — [LexiBox](https://www.lexibox.in/ra/bse-clarification-with-respect-to-advertisement-code-for-research-analysts-ras/); [Mehta & Mehta](https://mehta-mehtaadvisory.com/sebi-update-clarification-with-respect-to-advertisement-code-for-research-analysts-ras/)
- **Enforcement example:** SEBI fined an investment adviser (Basant Maheshwari Wealth Advisers) ₹4 lakh over "sensational" YouTube captions that breached the ad code — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/ad-code-breach-sebi-imposes-fine-of-rs-4-lakh-on-basant-maheshwari-wealth-125032601073_1.html). SEBI has also told portfolio managers to remove exaggerated ads (June 2025) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/stock-market-news/sebi-asks-portfolio-managers-to-remove-exaggerated-advertisements-125061700774_1.html)

**Past performance and accuracy claims**
- Under the original code, IAs and RAs could not show past performance or risk-return metrics unless a verification agency had verified them — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d1aafbb4-64df-4677-ab75-64b73b6faa14)
- Interim arrangement (SEBI circular of 30 Oct 2025, HO/38/12/11(1)2025-MIRSD-POD/I/73/2025): IAs and RAs may share past performance certified by an ICAI or ICMAI member, but **only on the client's specific request and on a one-to-one basis**. It must carry a disclaimer that PaRRVA has not verified it and that past performance does not guarantee future results. Two years after PaRRVA starts operating, only PaRRVA-verified data may be shared — [SEBI circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/oct-2025/ease-of-doing-business-interim-arrangement-for-certified-past-performance-of-investment-advisers-and-research-analysts-prior-to-operationalisation-of-past-risk-and-return-verification-agency-parrv-_97556.html); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/sebi-permits-interim-sharing-of-certified-past-performance-by-advisers-and-analysts-before-parrva-launch-1436294); [Legality Simplified](https://www.legalitysimplified.com/sebi-introduces-interim-framework-for-certified-past-performance-of-investment-advisers-and-research-analysts/)
- **PaRRVA:** SEBI's chairman launched the pilot in Dec 2025. CARE Ratings (the PaRRVA) and NSE (the PaRRVA Data Centre) launched it commercially on 4 May 2026. It computes about 50 risk-return metrics from exchange and clearing-corporation data. IAs, RAs and trading members submit their recommendations by API or file upload — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/care-ratings-in-collaboration-with-nse-launches-parrva-platform-126050401090_1.html); [Fintech Biz News](https://www.fintechbiznews.com/finserv-credit-bureaus-rating-agencies/parrvas-commercial-launch-by-nse-careedge-ratings); [careparrva.com](https://careparrva.com/)

**Payment and verification signals (India)**
- From 1 Oct 2025, SEBI-registered intermediaries that collect investor money, including brokers, IAs and RAs, must use exclusive "@valid" UPI handles. Payers see a green-triangle "thumbs-up" icon. The "SEBI Check" tool lets investors look up an intermediary by name, UPI handle or registration number — [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/sebi-mandates-exclusive-valid-upi-handles-for-registered-intermediaries-from-oct-1-480010-2025-06-11); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/10/04/sebi-introduces-verified-upi-handles-and-sebi-check-to-safeguard-investors/)

**US analogues (brief)**
- SEC: testimonials are allowed with disclosures, and hypothetical and backtested performance needs policies and disclosure of assumptions — [SEC guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing). AI claims must be accurate, as the Delphia and Global Predictions cases show — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/240320-sec-targets-ai-washing-with-two-new-settled-cases). FINRA 2210 has historically barred projections, with an amendment pending — [Quest CE](https://www.questce.com/finra-proposes-allowing-performance-projections-under-rule-2210/)

### Inferences
- **Remove or avoid on the landing page:** "guaranteed/assured profit", "risk-free", "X% accuracy / win rate", "₹X turned into ₹Y" hero numbers, "best/No. 1/leading/India's first/world's first", star ratings or testimonials about profits, influencer endorsements, referral contests with cash prizes, the SEBI logo, and "free" when an upgrade is required. Accuracy percentages count as past-performance and risk-return metrics. For an RA/IA they belong only on a PaRRVA-verified page, or in one-to-one sharing on request, not in a public hero.
- **Backtests and Mock Mode results** can be presented as a feature ("test ideas on historical data") but should not be presented as proof of returns. Label them "hypothetical/simulated, not indicative of future results". Law firms should confirm whether the Ad Code's "projections" ban treats backtest charts as projections.
- **Testimonials:** the Indian code bans "misleading or deceptive" testimonials, not all testimonials, but any testimonial about returns is very likely to count as a performance claim. Safer testimonials focus on usability ("I finally understand why a stock is flagged"). Legal review is needed.
- **Include in the footer or trust band:** the SEBI registration number and category, the exact registered name, the standard risk warning in 10pt or larger, the "registration does not guarantee returns" disclaimer, the AI-usage disclosure, the partner broker name and exchange algo empanelment (if applicable), a grievance and SCORES link, an "@valid UPI / verify us on SEBI Check" callout, and a link to a PaRRVA-verified performance page once one exists.
- **Finfluencer rule:** paid Instagram or YouTube creator promotion must use SEBI-registered creators, or creators on a "specified digital platform". Unregistered finfluencers making return claims cannot be used. FinStocks' existing Instagram reel marketing should be audited.

### Gaps
- The verbatim clause list of the Apr 2023 SEBI circular could not be retrieved. Items such as font size, audio/video disclaimer speed, and required elements like address and contact were sourced from secondary summaries.
- The specific templates from IAASB and RAASB for presenting certified past performance were not found.
- Whether an exchange-empanelled algo provider that is not an RA falls under the IA/RA Advertisement Code or only under broker and exchange ad rules is unclear. Legal review is needed.

## 3. Retail-investor psychology: trust barriers and credibility signals

### Takeaway
Indian retail investors are aware of markets but cautious and distrustful. The main barriers are fear of loss, lack of trust and complexity. The stock-tips space is heavily associated with WhatsApp and Telegram scams and with F&O losses. Globally, most retail investors still prefer human advice to AI. The signals that reliably reduce these barriers are verifiable regulation (a registration number that can be checked in SEBI Check, @valid UPI), explainability (showing why the AI suggests something), independently verified track records (PaRRVA), visible humans behind the product, and low-risk trial paths such as paper trading.

### Cited Findings
**Risk aversion and barriers (India)**
- The SEBI Investor Survey 2025 covered more than 90,000 households. 63% are aware of at least one securities product, but only about 9.5% invest (15% urban, 6% rural). Direct equity awareness is 49% with 5.3% penetration — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/two-thirds-of-domestic-households-aware-of-securities-products-sebi-survey-126012100349_1.html); [ScanX](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/stocks/sebi-investor-survey-2025-63-households-aware-of-market-products-only-9-5-actually-invest/30475080)
- About 80% of households, including 79% of Gen-Z households, prefer capital preservation to higher returns — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/indian-households-including-gen-z-remain-risk-averse-sebi-survey-125093001479_1.html); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/amp/story/invest/equity/sebi-survey-2025-only-1-on-10-indian-households-invest-in-stock-market-despite-high-awareness)
- The main barriers to participation are complex products, fear of losses, lack of trust, and low financial literacy — [Finnovate summary](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/sebi-investor-survey-2025-insights); [Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/sebi-investor-survey-2025-mapping)

**Loss experience among active traders**
- More than 91% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY25. Net losses rose 41% to ₹1,05,603 crore, from ₹74,812 crore in FY24. The sample was about 96 lakh traders at the top 13 brokers — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html). SEBI's earlier study found 93% lost money over FY22–FY24, with aggregate losses above ₹1.8 lakh crore — [SEBI press release](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html)

**Scam fear**
- I4C data show about ₹4,636 crore lost to stock-market trading scams in the first nine months of 2024, making it the most common type of cyber fraud. The typical pattern is an unknown WhatsApp or Telegram group, followed by "expert" tips, a fake trading app showing fake profits, and withdrawal fees. I4C flags "guaranteed returns" and "VIP stock tips" as red flags and advises downloading apps only from official stores — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/news/i4c-warns-investors-against-fake-whatsapp-stock-tips-fake-trading-apps); [Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/india/fake-profits-real-losses-the-new-investment-fraud-sweeping-whatsapp-and-telegram-groups)
- Losses in individual cases run into crores, for example ₹1.23 crore (Mumbai) and ₹1.77 crore (Mysuru) — [The420.in](https://the420.in/mumbai-whatsapp-trading-scam-1-23-crore-fake-app-sebi-forgery/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fmysuru%2Fmysuru-man-loses-rs-177-crore-in-fake-trading-app-scam-3855312). The government issued a further warning about WhatsApp and Telegram investment scams in July 2026 — [The News Mill](https://thenewsmill.com/2026/07/india-warns-against-investment-scams-on-whatsapp-and-telegram/)
- SEBI created @valid UPI and SEBI Check specifically because unregistered entities were collecting investor funds — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/10/04/sebi-introduces-verified-upi-handles-and-sebi-check-to-safeguard-investors/)

**Trust in AI and robo-advice**
- CFA Institute Investor Trust Study: 74% of retail investors trust human advice more than robo advice (73% in 2020). Only 39% of retail investors would consider AI-driven funds, against 84% of institutional investors. Investors under 44, especially millennials, trust robo and digital tools more — [CFA Institute trust report](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/-/media/documents/survey/cfa_investor_trust_global_report.pdf); [Hubbis](https://hubbis.com/news/cfa-institute-trust-study-finds-levels-of-trust-in-financial-services-reach-all-time-high). The same study series (2022) found Indian investors' trust in financial services was the highest of 15 markets — [ThePrint/ANI](https://theprint.in/ani-press-releases/indian-investors-trust-in-financial-services-is-highest-amongst-15-markets-cfa-institutes-2022-investor-trust-study/930243/). The exact survey year of the 74% and 39% figures was not confirmed from the snippet.
- Algorithm aversion, the tendency to discount advice from algorithms, is a documented barrier. In qualitative studies, investors called opaque systems a "black box" and adoption intent fell. Transparency about decision logic, explanations of portfolio changes, and the ability to monitor changes were named as trust requirements — [Jönköping Univ. thesis (DiVA)](https://hj.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:2024112/FULLTEXT01.pdf); [arXiv experimental study on XAI and algorithmic advisors](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.02555)
- Perceived personalisation, interactivity, autonomy and algorithm transparency reduced overconfidence and loss-aversion biases and increased robo-advisor use — [Cogent Economics & Finance, 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23322039.2025.2571403)
- Negative AI attitudes predict resistance to robo-advisors — [Frontiers in AI, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2025.1623534/full). Customer trust and satisfaction drivers for robo-advisers are discussed in [FPA Journal, Aug 2024](https://www.financialplanningassociation.org/learning/publications/journal/AUG24-customer-trust-and-satisfaction-robo-adviser-technology-OPEN)

### Inferences
- The scam playbook uses guaranteed returns, VIP tips, screenshots of profits, pressure to act now, and off-store apps. Any landing page element that looks like it (profit screenshots, countdown timers, "join our Telegram") will trigger scam pattern-matching. The page should visibly do the opposite: explain, verify and slow down.
- Credibility signals to prioritise, roughly in order:
  1. A regulatory identity that can be checked, with a "Verify on SEBI Check" link and the @valid UPI badge.
  2. The broker partner and the fact that money stays in the user's own demat or broker account (the site title "on your own ..." suggests this). Say plainly that FinStocks never holds the user's money.
  3. Explainability: a sample AI rationale card ("why this stock / what could go wrong"), plus an AI-disclosure panel.
  4. Visible founder and team with LinkedIn links and credentials, including any NISM-certified RA.
  5. Risk-first framing: stop-losses, position limits, and the ability to pause Autopilot.
  6. Mock Mode as the low-risk first step.
  7. A PaRRVA-verified track record once available.
- The page should say plainly that AI is fallible and that a human stays in control. This fits the 74% who prefer human advice and matches SEBI's position that the registered entity, not the AI, is accountable.

### Gaps
- No India-specific survey on retail trust in AI stock advice was found. The CFA data are global, and NSE Market Pulse was not reached.
- No hard data was found on landing-page conversion effects of specific trust badges in Indian fintech.

## 4. User personas: beginner vs active trader vs long-term investor

### Takeaway
The data point to three distinct groups. First-time and cautious investors are the largest untapped pool: aware but not investing, preferring capital safety, and put off by complexity and distrust. Active and F&O traders are numerous (tens of lakhs), mostly losing money, and heavily targeted by scams and by SEBI's algo rules. Long-term investors look for discipline and diversification. Each group needs different proof and different reassurance. No source provided validated persona research specific to AI trading tools in India, so the persona details below are inferences.

### Cited Findings
- Beginners and non-investors: 63% are aware but only 9.5% participate. Complexity, fear of loss, lack of trust and low literacy are the barriers. About 80%, including Gen Z, prefer capital preservation — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/indian-households-including-gen-z-remain-risk-averse-sebi-survey-125093001479_1.html); [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/sebi-investor-survey-2025-insights)
- Active traders: about 96 lakh F&O traders in SEBI's sample and more than 91% lost money in FY25. Individual participation in F&O fell about 20% after SEBI's 2024–25 interventions — [Business Standard (losses)](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html); [Business Standard (20% decline)](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/individual-investors-shrink-20-percent-fo-segment-125070800801_1.html)
- Algo-curious traders are told to rely on back-tested data rather than hype — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/algo-trading-by-retail-investors-trust-back-tested-data-not-hype-125072901081_1.html)
- Younger investors (under 44) are more open to robo and digital tools and to personalised products and nudges — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/-/media/documents/survey/cfa_investor_trust_global_report.pdf)
- Mutual funds lead penetration at 6.7% against 5.3% for direct equity, which suggests long-term and SIP-style investors are an important existing base — [ScanX](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/stocks/sebi-investor-survey-2025-63-households-aware-of-market-products-only-9-5-actually-invest/30475080)

### Inferences
- **Beginner (first-time, often 20s–30s, small ticket such as "₹10,000"):** wants plain language, safety, and to learn without losing money. Page needs: Mock Mode first, "your money stays in your broker account", explanations in simple terms, education without tips, minimums and fees shown upfront, and visible regulation. Avoid jargon (alpha, Sharpe) and anything that looks like a get-rich pitch.
- **Active trader / algo-curious:** wants speed, control, backtesting depth, strategy transparency, execution quality and cost. Is sceptical of "AI" hype and has probably been burned before. Page needs: white-box logic and editable strategies, clear risk controls (stop-loss, max drawdown, kill switch), exchange algo-ID and empanelment, a broker list, all charges disclosed (required under the algo framework), and backtest methodology with a hypothetical-results label. Do not present F&O as easy money. Given the 91% loss statistic, an honest line about risk may build credibility with this group.
- **Long-term investor:** wants discipline, diversification, rebalancing, low churn and research depth. Page needs: rebalancing logic explained, a portfolio-level view, goal-based framing, and a verified long-horizon track record through PaRRVA rather than short-term "hit rate" figures.
- Across all three, the hero should lead with the product mechanism (prompt → AI rationale → you approve or Autopilot within the limits you set) rather than outcomes, because outcome claims are the most restricted.

### Gaps
- No Indian primary research segmenting AI-trading-tool users was found. The personas are inferred from SEBI survey and F&O data plus global CFA trust data, and should be validated with FinStocks' own user analytics and interviews.
- NSE Market Pulse demographic data (age and city-tier split of new investors) was not retrieved and would strengthen the personas.
